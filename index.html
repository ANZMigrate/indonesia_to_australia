National Innovation visa / subclass 858

Permanent residence for exceptional talent.

The National Innovation visa (NIV) is Australia's exclusive permanent visa for people with an internationally recognised record of exceptional and outstanding achievement. It is aimed at global researchers, entrepreneurs, innovative investors, senior professionals, athletes and creatives who will contribute to Australia's future prosperity.

Why the NIV is different

No points test, no occupation list, no sponsor.

The NIV is assessed on the strength of your record, not on age points or a skills list. The question is whether your achievements place you among the best in your field internationally, and whether you will contribute to Australia.

PROutcome

Permanent from day one

You become a permanent resident on grant, with a five-year travel facility and a pathway to citizenship if eligible.

  • Live, work and study anywhere in Australia
  • Medicare and family sponsorship rights

You do not need an Australian employer to sponsor you, and no job offer is required, although you must show you can establish yourself in your field.

  • Unrestricted work rights
  • Suits founders, researchers and executives

No age limit

Applicants of any age can apply. If you are under 18 or aged 55 or over, you must also show you would be of exceptional benefit to the Australian community.

  • Benefit must be ongoing and sustained
  • Evidence such as a senior role or business plan
ENEnglish

No English test score

Functional English is required for applicants aged 18 or over, or a second instalment charge can be paid instead of providing that evidence.

  • No IELTS band or points target
  • Second instalment payable only before grant

Your partner and dependent children can be included in the application, subject to health and character requirements.

  • Members can be added before a decision
  • Additional charge per family member

Apply onshore or offshore

You can be in or outside Australia when you apply and when the visa is granted, but not in immigration clearance.

  • Onshore applicants need an eligible visa status
  • 491 and 494 holders need 3 years on that visa

Core requirements

Exceptional, international, recent and continuing.

The legal test is demanding. A strong CV is not enough on its own. Your record must be internationally recognised in a profession, a sport, the arts, or academia and research, and it must still be active.

What the Department must be satisfied of

Your achievements place you among the best in your area.

The record must show superior abilities and achievements compared with others in your field, acclaim as exceptional wherever that field is practised, and sustained achievement that is unlikely to diminish in the near future.

  • An internationally recognised record of exceptional and outstanding achievement
  • Recent achievements that show you are still active and prominent
  • A field that is recognised in Australia and has international standing
  • A benefit to the Australian community, whether economic, social, cultural or in raising Australia's standing
  • The ability to find work or become established in your field in Australia without difficulty
  • A nominator with a national reputation in your field, using Form 1000
  • Health, character and functional English (or the second instalment)

Indicators of exceptional achievement

Evidence that speaks for itself.

Ministerial Direction 120 lists the indicators the Department looks for. They are guidance, not a checklist, and each case is assessed on its overall merits. In practice, the strongest files combine several indicators with independent proof of impact.

01Top of field

International awards

Awards that place you at the very top of your discipline worldwide, such as a Nobel Prize, Fields Medal, Turing Award, Pulitzer Prize or Olympic gold medal.

  • Highest priority for invitation
  • Can apply in any sector
02Research funding

National research grants

Competitive national or international grants, such as the Australian Research Council, UKRI, the European Commission or the US National Science Foundation.

  • Named investigator on the grant
  • Internal university funding is not enough
03Academic influence

PhD with thought leadership

A PhD combined with influence in the field, such as recent papers in top-ranked journals, a high h-index for your career stage, or a research degree from a top-100 university.

  • Early career benchmark: h-index of around 14
  • Times Higher Education top 100 as a guide
04Recognition

Keynote appearances

Recent keynote or invited addresses at high-profile international conferences in your field.

  • External industry or academic events
  • Internal company events do not count
05Innovation

Patents and intellectual property

Recognised intellectual property attributed to you, such as relevant international patents for cutting-edge products or technology.

  • Named inventor or clear key involvement
  • Granted patents carry the most weight
06Earnings

High income

Current earnings, or a written Australian job offer, at or above the Fair Work high income threshold in your area of achievement. The threshold is AUD 190,100 from 1 July 2026.

  • Base salary equivalent is the usual measure
  • Income outside your field does not count

Other supporting evidence can include senior technical or leadership roles at reputed organisations, Academic Level D or E appointments, leading a company to IPO, advisory board appointments, statements from peak bodies, and international media coverage of your work.

How to build the evidence

Priority sectors

Your sector shapes your priority.

Invitations are issued in priority order. After top-of-field award holders and government agency nominees, the Department prioritises candidates with exceptional achievements in Tier One sectors, followed by Tier Two sectors.

Priority 3 / Tier One

Critical Technologies, Health Industries, Renewables and Low Emission Technologies

Includes AI, robotics, autonomous systems, cyber security, quantum, photonics, advanced ICT and manufacturing, biotechnology, genomics, precision medicine, AI in health, wearable devices, renewable energy, green hydrogen, green metals, carbon storage and critical minerals processing.

Our assessment benchmark: at least one strong indicator, supported by evidence of achievement in the sector.

Priority 4 / Tier Two

Agri-food and AgTech, Defence and Space, Education, FinTech, Infrastructure and Transport, Resources

Includes food technology and biosecurity, propulsion and space systems engineering, senior university leadership and Level D or E academics, blockchain, cloud and financial data science, large transport infrastructure projects, and automation and value-adding in resources.

Our assessment benchmark: ideally at least two indicators relevant to the sector, supported by evidence of achievement.

Priority 1 is reserved for recipients of international top-of-field awards. Priority 2 covers candidates nominated on Form 1000 by an expert Australian Government, State or Territory Government agency, where investment, entrepreneurial and community achievements may also be considered.

Building the file

Prove the achievement, not just the job title.

Decision makers look for independent proof that your work is recognised beyond your employer. Titles and salaries help, but third-party evidence of impact is what separates an exceptional record from a successful career.

See the application sequence
Evidence of achievement in the sector

Show who recognised your work, and where.

Every indicator claimed in the EOI must be supported by documents in the visa application. Keep the claims consistent from EOI to lodgement.

  • Media articles, interviews and industry mentions of your work
  • Case studies showing products, projects or research you led
  • Award certificates, grant letters and patent registrations
  • Publication records, citation metrics and Google Scholar profile
  • Conference programmes confirming keynote or invited speaker roles
  • Employment contracts and payslips showing role and salary
  • Reference letters from recognised experts in your field
  • A statement of your plans and opportunities in Australia

How the process works

The NIV is invitation only.

You cannot lodge a visa application until the Department invites you. Invitation rounds are held monthly. From April to June 2026, the Department received 2,166 EOIs and issued 248 invitations, most of them to Tier One candidates. An invitation is not a pre-assessment, so the application must still prove every claim.

Frequently asked questions

Common NIV questions.

Short answers to the questions applicants ask most often. Settings can change, so always confirm current requirements before lodging.

Do I need a job offer or employer sponsor?

No. The NIV does not require sponsorship or a job offer. You do need to show you can find work or become established in your field in Australia, and a written job offer at or above the high income threshold can count as an indicator.

Who can be my nominator?

An Australian citizen, Australian permanent resident, eligible New Zealand citizen or Australian organisation with a national reputation in your field. They sign Form 1000 to attest to your record, and it must be attached when you lodge. Nomination by an expert government agency places you in Priority 2.

How many indicators do I need?

There is no fixed number, because the indicators are guidance and not a checklist. As a working benchmark, a Tier One candidate may succeed with one strong indicator, while a Tier Two candidate should ideally show at least two, each backed by evidence of recognised achievement in the sector.

What if my field is not a priority sector?

You can still submit an EOI, but candidates outside the priority sectors are considered after Priorities 1 to 4. Exceptions include top-of-field award holders, athletes and creatives, and experienced entrepreneurs or investors, particularly with government agency nomination.

How much does the visa cost?

The main applicant charge is from AUD 6,235, with additional charges for family members. A second instalment of AUD 4,890 applies to each applicant aged 18 or over with less than functional English. Health checks, police certificates and biometrics are extra.

How long does it take?

Timing depends on your priority level, the completeness of your application and the time taken for health, character and security checks. Higher priority candidates are invited and processed first.
